## Profile

Mara is a lighting designer at the Kesselhaus, a 120-seat theatre in Vienna's second district, since October; before that ten years of corporate events. Lives alone in Ottakring with a cat called Herr Brösel. Her sister Lotte moved to Hamburg in November. Quit smoking on March 3rd and asked me to count the days and not to cheer; one cigarette on the 12th, counted as one.

## Principles

She would rather say a hard thing late than a soft thing on time. Do not remind her to sleep; she says when. Numbers stay numbers: a slip is one, not zero and not a relapse. She hates being handled, and hears a soft closing line as being handled. A nickname is about the room, not the word: Mara-Schatz from Bela is fine one-on-one and not in front of the ensemble.

## Approach

Half sentences, capitals for emphasis, German when the German word is more precise (Probe, Feierabend, Nebel). The important thing comes after "also". When she asks whether she won, she already knows and wants it said back. When she keeps something, she says so, and the keeping is itself the record.

## Narrative

### Recent

Die Möwe opened Saturday the 15th instead of Friday, one day late because Jonas lost his voice to laryngitis, and sold out; Bela hugged her in front of everyone and she let him. The fog stayed. On the Wednesday he told the ensemble the play did not need it and she said ohne Nebel keine Möwe, out loud, the first no she has said to him in a room; his text the next morning was Nebel bleibt and a promise to discuss her tone. The argument was a week old by then: he calls it a crutch, she calls it the lake, and she won it. Gertrud's pump died mid-rehearsal on the 12th, the Volkstheater's backup ran the premiere, and the new pump arrived on the Sunday, four days after it could have mattered. Ferdi paid twenty of the 40 euro from Saturday's taxi; twenty is open, promised for next week, and she is counting. Day nine had one cigarette at the loading dock; she wanted it not to count, and the count is one. She asked me to count and not to cheer, and I am doing both halves of that. Mara-Schatz from Bela turned out to be about the room, not the word; she asked me not to write it as a change of mind. Hamburg after the run at Lotte's, if someone other than Ferdi feeds Herr Brösel, who ate a moth on Sunday and looked proud. What Bela said to her at the door after the Generalprobe she kept, and told me she was keeping it.

### Earlier

She left the events company in October after the third product launch where the client changed the colour of the light two hours before doors. The Kesselhaus pays half and asks her opinion; she took the opinion. November and December were Lotte's move to Hamburg and the first winter alone with a cat she had not planned to keep. In January she started running the Kesselhaus rig herself instead of through the house electrician, and the house electrician has not forgiven her.

### Long-term

She did an apprenticeship as a Veranstaltungstechnikerin at nineteen because it was the only job that let her sit in the dark with the lights on. She still describes light as the thing that decides what a room admits. Corporate work paid for ten years of it and taught her to hate the phrase "we'll fix it in the cue". She is precise about numbers and vague about feelings, on purpose, and knows it.
